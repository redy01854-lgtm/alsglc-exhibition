Why does this issue matter?

What three groups told us

Each group answered five questions. Every card below is one respondent's answer, shown as written.

Across all three groups

Where the answers meet, and where they pull apart

Free tuition could lift a cost

Students and parents both pointed to money. Private tutoring is expensive, and school-run sessions would free up the family budget.

It would cut monthly private tuition costs, freeing up money for books, internet access, or basic household needs.Parent

Time and rest are the main worry

Every group raised it. Sessions after a full school day could eat into homework, hobbies, family time and sleep.

Sometimes, there's barely any free time to do other activities or rest.Student

Schools may already have what it takes

Teachers described using the lounge and teacher room, and existing materials. A parent noted school teachers already know the syllabus.

Yes, because they use lounge for 30 minutes free tuition.Teacher

Who should qualify is still open

Parents suggested low income, proven academic gaps, diagnostic tests and attendance. A student argued support should rest on need alone.

Academic support should be based purely on educational need rather than income level to ensure equal learning opportunities.Student

Small comforts make sessions workable

Respondents asked for meals or a snack break, small classes, patient teachers, a safe way home and sessions right after school instead of late at night.

Manipulation Methods

Manipulation techniques in the answers

People do not always mean to mislead, but everyday opinions can still lean on tricks of language and selection. Here are four techniques as they appear in real answers from our survey. This is our reading, and another reader might judge some of them differently.

Cherry-picking

Naming only the facts that help one side.

Student 2 · Q3
Their answer“It's free and it provides me knowledge”
Why it fitsLists only the benefit. It leaves out the cost to the school and the student's own time.
Teacher 3 · Q5
Their answer“no since they already have the materials for it”
Why it fitsCounts the materials but skips teacher time, electricity and supervision, which other teachers raised.
Parent 1 · Q4
Their answer“Yes, because school teachers already know the syllabus and can target the exact foundational topics a student missed.”
Why it fitsGives the strongest advantage and says nothing about tired teachers or students after a long day.

Loaded language

Choosing emotional words that steer the reader's feelings.

Parent 3 · Q2
Their answer“My biggest worry would be my child getting overtired, hungry, and burned out after a long school day”
Why it fits“Overtired, hungry, and burned out” is a stack of strong words that paints the sessions as harmful.
Parent 3 · Q5
Their answer“It would take a huge weight off our monthly budget”
Why it fits“Huge weight” makes the saving sound heavier than the answer shows. No amount is given.
Parent 2 · Q1
Their answer“ensuring spots go to struggling learners who will actually show up and put in the effort”
Why it fits“Actually show up and put in the effort” suggests other struggling students would not.

Exaggeration

Making a claim bigger or more certain than the evidence allows.

Student 3 · Q1
Their answer“Yes, it can decrease the rate of dropouts in schools”
Why it fitsA big outcome stated as fact, with no evidence given for dropout rates.
Teacher 3 · Q1
Their answer“yes, everyone will have same opportunity because its free”
Why it fitsAssumes free means equal. Time, transport and tiredness could still stop some students attending.
Teacher 1 · Q5
Their answer“yes, because of their electricity bills”
Why it fitsTurns one running cost into a reason the whole programme creates unnecessary expenses.

Omission

Leaving out the detail needed to judge the answer.

Teacher 1 · Q1
Their answer“yes, because same education”
Why it fitsDoes not say what is the same or how free tuition makes it so.
Student 3 · Q2
Their answer“No i dont”
Why it fitsGives no reason and no example, so the reader cannot check it.
Student 2 · Q5
Their answer“I believe it should be free for everyone but I believe schools wouldnt agree as it takes school resources to do tuitions”
Why it fitsNames the problem of resources but not who would pay or how.
Source Evaluation

How far can we trust these answers?

We checked our survey answers against four tests: are they relevant, reliable, sufficient and free of bias.

Relevant

Mostly relevant

Do the answers speak to the question of free tuition for struggling students?

  • Every question was written for the exhibition track, and most answers address it directly.
  • The three groups are the people free tuition would affect: students who attend, teachers who run it and parents who live with the results.
  • Some answers drift. One teacher answered the cost question with "students can find their teachers in the teacher room or lounge during recess or after school".

To improve: keep each answer tied to its question and ask a follow-up when it drifts.

Reliable

Opinions, not proof

Can we trust what respondents told us, and can it be checked?

  • The answers are first-hand views from people who know the situation, so they are good evidence of what people think and experience.
  • Facts inside the answers are unchecked. A student said free tuition "can decrease the rate of dropouts", with no source.
  • Our three secondary sources (Stanford NSSA, J-PAL and Education Research for Action) can be used to test claims like this.

To improve: compare each factual claim with a secondary source before we repeat it.

Sufficient

Not enough alone

Is there enough evidence to reach a conclusion?

  • We have 9 responses, 3 from each group.
  • With so few, one person changes the picture a lot. Teachers split 1 to 2 on extra expenses.
  • The answers show themes worth exploring. They cannot show what most students, teachers or parents think.

To improve: survey more people in each group and add schools from different areas.

Bias

Bias possible

Could anything have pushed the answers one way?

  • Self-interest: students gain free help, parents save money and teachers take on more work, and each answer reflects that.
  • Question wording: asking whether free tuition could create "unnecessary expenses" leans toward a yes.
  • Who we asked: we have not recorded how respondents were chosen, so the sample may be people close to us who share similar views.

To improve: use neutral wording, choose respondents more randomly and say how they were chosen.
